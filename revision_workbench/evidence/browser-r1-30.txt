Beyond the in-domain MazurkaBL evaluation, Section~\ref{ssec:audiodyn_cross_dataset} reports a test-only cross-dataset evaluation. For each architecture and front-end variant, the five MazurkaBL fold models are all evaluated on Vienna4x22 and Batik-plays-Mozart without fine-tuning. For the multitask variants, the external table selects the fold with the best dynamic F1 on the target corpus and reports the other task scores from the same checkpoint. For the single-task variants, each task has its own fold models, so the best fold is selected separately for the corresponding task. This protocol is intended as a reproducible transfer diagnostic: it asks whether a MazurkaBL-trained fold checkpoint can be reused on an external classical-piano corpus, while the main MazurkaBL table remains the within-corpus benchmark reported as mean and standard deviation over all folds. The per-fold outputs are retained so that the selected fold can be traced.

第5章 §5.7.4 · Chapter_5/chap5.tex:392–392 · 保留相关原文
The cross-dataset results make this distinction more concrete. If score dynamics were merely a continuous loudness variable relabelled into five fixed bins, an out-of-domain corpus with compatible audio quality would mainly cause smooth calibration errors. Instead, Vienna4x22 shows that the symbolic prior itself matters: a passage that is moderate in acoustic intensity may be notated as \textit{p}, \textit{mf}, or \textit{f} depending on the local phrase, edition, and corpus convention. A model trained on MazurkaBL carries a learned expectation about how often each of these labels should occur, and this expectation can become wrong even when the metrical grid remains reasonably stable. The model is therefore learning an acoustic, metrical, and editorial mapping at the same time.
